Curious Male Rabbit Names

He's already investigated every corner of the room, mapped the perimeter of the garden, and discovered exactly which drawer holds the treats. A curious rabbit is a force of nature — nose going, ears swivelling, eyes wide with the absolute certainty that something interesting is just around the next corner. These names are drawn from explorers, wanderers, thinkers, and the great literary rabbits who went boldly down the rabbit hole first.

The Names

Pip

Old English — A small seed with everything it needs inside it; compact, lively, and full of potential

Cosmo

Greek — Order and the whole of the universe; a name for one determined to explore all of it

Ptolemy

Greek — Named for the great astronomer who mapped the heavens with tireless curiosity

Fable

Latin — A story waiting to be told; a life lived as a series of small, wonderful discoveries

Darwin

Old English — Dear friend; also the naturalist who looked closer at the world than anyone before him

Ludo

Latin — I play; a name built entirely from the joy of exploring just to see what happens

Thistle

Old English — A wild plant that pushes through everywhere uninvited, bristling with character

Rafferty

Irish Gaelic — Prosperity wielder; a grand name for a rabbit with very big plans

Noodle

German — Playful and soft; a name for the one who wriggles into absolutely everything

Juniper

Latin — A hardy wild shrub that grows in unexpected places; resourceful and quietly adventurous

Barnabas

Hebrew/Aramaic — Son of encouragement; a warm, bumbling presence who means well in all his mischief

Quill

Old German — A writing instrument; sharp, purposeful, and leaving a mark wherever it goes

Meriwether

Old English — Happy wanderer; named for the explorer who mapped unknown territories with wonder

Fennel

Old English/Latin — A feathery wild herb that sprawls outward; aromatic, wilful, and untidy in the best way

Hobbit

Old English — A small creature of great courage who ventures far beyond his comfortable front door

Alistair

Scottish Gaelic — Defender of men; a serious name worn by a rabbit who takes his investigations very seriously

Wren

Old English — A tiny bird with an outsize voice that explores every hedgerow without hesitation

Copernicus

Latin/Polish — The astronomer who rearranged everything we thought we knew about the world

Clover

Old English — A meadow herb sought out with patience and a very thorough nose

Fletcher

Old English/French — Arrow maker; one who is always pointed somewhere new and moving there quickly
