Talkative Male Parrot Names

He has opinions, and he will share them. Loudly. Repeatedly. At six in the morning if the mood takes him. A talkative parrot is one of nature's great conversationalists — part mimic, part philosopher, part heckler, and entirely convinced that what he has to say is worth hearing. These names are drawn from history's great orators, legendary talkers, theatrical figures, and the colourful language of sailors who spent too long at sea with a bird on their shoulder.

The Names

Cicero

Latin — Rome's greatest orator; a man whose words could move crowds, topple governments, and fill any room

Prospero

Latin/Italian — The sorcerer of Shakespeare's Tempest; a man of vast vocabulary and very strong feelings about everything

Bluster

Old English — To speak with loud, gusty, unstoppable force; the sound of a parrot who has found his volume

Voltaire

French — Named for the razor-tongued philosopher who had something sharp to say about absolutely everything

Figaro

Italian/Spanish — The quick-witted, fast-talking barber of opera; always scheming, always chattering, never still

Clamour

Latin — A loud and persistent outcry; the natural state of a parrot who feels he has not yet been heard

Socrates

Greek — The philosopher who questioned everything and everyone, relentlessly, until they begged him to stop

Bardolph

Old English — Bright wolf; a Shakespearean character of great noise, colour, and cheerful disreputable energy

Calypso

Greek — She who conceals; a musical style of pointed commentary that says exactly what it means in song

Señor

Spanish — Mister; a title of self-appointed authority worn by a bird who considers himself in charge of the household

Orion

Greek — Great hunter of the heavens; a name of mythic scale for a bird whose voice fills the entire sky

Boatswain

Old English — The ship's officer responsible for all the noise and rigging; a seafaring name worthy of any pirate's parrot

Mercutio

Latin/Italian — Shakespeare's most dazzling talker; witty, wild, and impossible to interrupt once he gets going

Kazoo

American English — A buzzing, penetrating instrument that produces sound with joyful disregard for anyone's preferences

Plato

Greek — Broad-shouldered; also the philosopher who recorded the words of others with great enthusiasm and at great length

Ruckus

American English — A lively commotion; the word that best describes what happens the moment he wakes up each morning

Horatio

Latin — The one who hears everything, remembers all of it, and cannot resist reporting it back at full volume

Palaver

Portuguese/English — Prolonged, elaborate talk that goes on considerably longer than anyone planned or hoped for

Ptolemy

Greek — Named for the great scholar who catalogued the known world with obsessive, exhaustive, vociferous thoroughness

Squawkins

Old English — From squawk; a name that describes both his primary mode of communication and his proudest achievement
